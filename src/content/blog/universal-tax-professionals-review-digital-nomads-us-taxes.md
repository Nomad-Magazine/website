## What Kind of Tax Help Do You Need?

It usually comes down to how you earn your money, where you spend your time and whether you have financial accounts or business interests outside the US. The table below gives a quick overview of some situations that may require additional attention.

| Your situation | What to consider |
| --- | --- |
| You have one US income source and live in one country | Your tax return may be relatively straightforward |
| You freelance or run your own business | You may need to account for business income, expenses and self-employment tax |
| You move between several countries each year | Keep careful records of where you've spent your time |
| You have foreign bank accounts or investments | Check whether additional US reporting applies |
| You own or operate a foreign company | You may have additional reporting requirements |
| You've missed previous US tax filings | Speak to a tax professional about your options before filing |

## Handling Foreign Accounts and Entities

Nomads frequently open local bank accounts for rent or daily spending in each country they work from.

Once combined foreign account balances cross the reporting threshold, additional filings come into play, and higher asset levels can trigger further disclosure requirements.

Nomads who set up a foreign company to bill clients, such as an Estonian e-Residency business, also need that entity reported correctly to avoid steep penalties for a missed filing.

Universal Tax Professionals also handles some of the less straightforward reporting requirements that can come with living and working abroad. That includes Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures for people who have fallen behind on previous returns, as well as Form 5471 for certain foreign corporations. The company also deals with PFIC reporting, which can become relevant when US taxpayers hold certain foreign investments, e.g. foreign mutual funds, ETFs, or investment accounts abroad.
